# 04 — Strategic Layer (the Kingdom Game)

The grand-strategy layer is where most playtime lives: ruling a faction,
developing your realm, and out-maneuvering rivals. It is **turn-based** (one
turn = one season, four turns = one year) with the option of a flowing
"real-time-ish" pace toggle for veterans.

## The map

- Stylized 3D map of China: **provinces → cities/commanderies → districts**,
  connected by roads, rivers, mountain passes, and ferry crossings.
- **Cities** are the unit of control: each has population, economy buildings,
  defenses, a garrison, and an assigned governor.
- **Terrain & chokepoints** matter strategically (Hangu Pass, the Yangtze,
  Sichuan's mountains) — geography shapes campaigns as it did historically.

## The turn loop

Each season you allocate your faction's **action points / command capacity**
(scaled by number of cities and officers) across:

1. **Develop** — build/upgrade farms, markets, walls, barracks, academies;
   land reclamation; population growth.
2. **Administer** — set tax rates, distribute grain, manage public order,
   handle disasters (floods, plagues, famine, locusts) and refugees.
3. **Military** — conscript/train troops, forge equipment, stockpile food,
   form armies, and launch campaigns.
4. **Personnel** — recruit, assign, reward, and promote officers; arrange
   marriages; manage loyalty.
5. **Diplomacy & Intrigue** — see below.

A turn ends with **resolution**: AI factions act, events fire, battles
initiated this turn are fought, the annals update.

## Economy

- **Resources:** Gold (treasury), Grain/Food (feeds armies & population),
  Population/Manpower (conscription pool), Materials (construction/siege),
  and abstract **Order** and **Morale** per city.
- Buildings form light **tech/upgrade trees** per city specialization (economic
  hub, fortress, military academy, trade port).
- **Trade routes** between your cities and neutral/allied factions generate gold
  and can be raided or blockaded.
- Over-taxing or starving cities tanks Order → riots, banditry, defections.

## Delegation & advisors (modern UX)

To serve both micro-managers and big-picture players:

- Assign a **governor** to a city and toggle **auto-manage** — the AI develops
  it per a policy you set (focus: economy / military / balanced).
- **Advisors** surface recommendations ("Famine looming in Chenliu — divert
  grain?") with one-click actions; you can act, ignore, or auto-resolve.
- A **delegation slider** scales how much the game plays itself, from
  full hands-on to "I only command armies and big decisions."

## Diplomacy

A reactive, character-aware diplomacy model:

- Actions: alliance, non-aggression pact, trade agreement, joint war, tribute,
  hostage exchange, dynastic marriage, vassalization, demand surrender.
- **Reputation & legitimacy:** honoring/breaking deals shifts how *every*
  faction treats you. The Han Emperor (while he lives) is a legitimacy token —
  "controlling the Emperor to command the lords" is a real strategy.
- AI factions have agendas and remember grudges; coalitions form against a
  runaway leader (a built-in catch-up/anti-snowball mechanic).

## Intrigue (deep dive)

Scheming is a first-class system, not a side menu:

- **Espionage:** plant spies in enemy cities to reveal troops, steal tech/gold,
  sabotage buildings, or open gates during a siege.
- **Subversion:** lower enemy officer loyalty, instigate defections, frame
  officers for treason, spread rumors to cause unrest.
- **Assassination & coups:** high-risk plots against enemy rulers/officers.
- **Counter-intelligence:** your spymaster defends against the same, and the AI
  schemes against you — paranoia is part of the fantasy.

## Events & decisions

- Seasonal **events** (natural disasters, defections, omens, story beats)
  present branching choices with meaningful, remembered consequences.
- **Imperial court events** (while the Han exists): receive titles, be ordered
  to campaign, or seize influence over the throne.

## Succession & continuity

- Your **ruler can die**; an heir inherits. Weak succession invites court
  factions to fracture or vassals to break away — a natural mid/late-game
  tension and a soft difficulty spike.
- Designating heirs, grooming them, and managing the dynasty is part of play.
