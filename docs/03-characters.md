# 03 — Characters & Officer System

The officer system is the heart of the game — directly descended from ROTK,
expanded with a Crusader-Kings-style living simulation.

## Roster

- **800+ named historical officers**, the full *Sanguo Yanyi* cast: Liu Bei,
  Guan Yu, Zhang Fei, Zhao Yun, Zhuge Liang, Cao Cao, Sima Yi, Sun Quan, Zhou
  Yu, Lü Bu, Diao Chan, Dong Zhuo, Yuan Shao, and hundreds more.
- Each has a portrait, a 3D character model, faction history, and a personality
  that drives AI behavior.
- **Custom officers / editor:** players can create officers (and import portraits)
  to inject into any scenario, ROTK-style.

## Core stats

Modernized version of ROTK's classic five attributes (1–100 each):

| Stat | Drives |
|------|--------|
| **Command (統)** | Army size led, morale, battlefield defense |
| **Might (武)** | Melee/duel power, frontline damage |
| **Intellect (智)** | Stratagems, plot success, tactical abilities |
| **Politics (政)** | City development, taxation, diplomacy, admin |
| **Charisma (魅)** | Recruitment, loyalty of subordinates, troop morale |

Derived/secondary values: **Loyalty**, **Ambition**, **Fame/Renown**, **Merit**,
**Age**, **Health**.

## Traits & talents

Beyond raw stats, officers carry **traits** that create identity and emergent
behavior:

- **Combat traits:** *Duelist*, *Cavalry Master*, *Naval Commander*,
  *Ambush Expert*, *Siege Engineer*.
- **Civil traits:** *Master Administrator*, *Silver Tongue*, *Spymaster*,
  *Logistician*.
- **Personality traits:** *Loyal*, *Ambitious*, *Cruel*, *Honorable*,
  *Cowardly*, *Reckless*, *Cautious* — these gate which schemes and dialogue
  options the officer engages in, and how the AI plays them.
- **Skill trees / specializations:** officers gain XP and unlock active
  abilities (e.g., Zhuge Liang's *Fire Stratagem*, Guan Yu's *Lone Rider*),
  giving an RPG progression layer absent from older ROTK titles.

## Roles & assignment

Officers are assigned to jobs, and assignment *is* the strategy game:

- **General** — leads an army unit in the field.
- **Governor / Magistrate** — runs a city's economy and order.
- **Advisor / Strategist** — boosts faction-wide actions and unlocks schemes.
- **Diplomat, Spy, Trainer, Merchant** — specialist civil roles.
- **Ruler & Heir** — the faction head; succession matters.

## Loyalty, ambition & the living court (the modern twist)

Officers are not static assets. They have inner lives:

- **Loyalty** rises with rewards, rank, victories, good relationships, and
  shared values; falls with neglect, defeats, slights, and better offers from
  rivals. Low-loyalty officers can **defect**, **leak intel**, or **rebel**.
- **Ambition** drives officers to seek promotions, fiefs, or — for the most
  ambitious (a Sima Yi, a Lü Bu) — to plot **usurpation** of your own faction.
- **Court factions:** officers form cliques (by region, clan, ideology). Court
  politics emerge — you manage rivalries and keep powerful blocs in check.
- **Schemes / intrigue:** sow discord between enemy officers, frame, bribe,
  assassinate, instigate defections, spread rumors. Both you and the AI plot.

## Relationships

A relationship graph connects officers and shapes the whole simulation:

- **Sworn brotherhood** (the Peach Garden Oath as the iconic example): bonded
  officers fight harder together and refuse to betray one another.
- **Marriage & bloodlines:** dynastic marriages cement alliances; children
  inherit traits and become the next generation of officers.
- **Friendship / rivalry / hatred:** affects co-deployment bonuses, defection
  targets, and event triggers.
- **Mentor/student, lord/retainer** historical bonds pre-seeded from the novel.

## Lifecycle: aging, generations & death

- Officers **age** over the campaign and die of old age, illness, battle wounds,
  duels, or execution — historically-anchored death years in Chronicle Mode,
  emergent in Free Mode.
- **New generations** are born and come of age, so a long campaign naturally
  refreshes its roster (Guan Yu → Guan Ping/Guan Xing, etc.), preventing the
  late-game roster collapse of older ROTK titles.
- Capturing enemy officers gives choices: **recruit, release, imprison, or
  execute** — each with reputation and relationship consequences.

## Why this matters

The officer simulation is the engine of stories. A defection at the wrong
moment, a brilliant strategist dying young, a sworn brother avenged — these are
the moments players remember and share, and they emerge from these systems
rather than being scripted.
