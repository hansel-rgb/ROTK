# 02 — Setting & Story

## Historical frame

The game covers the fall of the Eastern Han and the Three Kingdoms period,
**184 AD (Yellow Turban Rebellion) to 280 AD (Jin reunification)**, the same
canvas as the ROTK series and the *Sanguo Yanyi* novel. The map is China:
provinces (州), commanderies, cities, passes, and rivers rendered as a stylized
3D realm.

We dramatize history in the **romance** tradition: real people and events, but
heroic framing — sworn brotherhoods, brilliant stratagems, duels at the gates,
loyalty unto death.

## Scenarios (campaign start points)

Like classic ROTK, the game ships with multiple historical start dates. Each
sets the initial map ownership, officer placement, and active story events.

| # | Scenario | Year | Hook |
|---|----------|------|------|
| 1 | **The Yellow Turban Rebellion** | 184 | Heroes first rise; Liu Bei, Cao Cao, Sun Jian begin as minor officers. The "rags to empire" start. |
| 2 | **Dong Zhuo's Tyranny** | 190 | The coalition of warlords against the tyrant; intrigue-heavy. |
| 3 | **The Contending Heroes** | 194 | Open free-for-all; classic "everyone for themselves." |
| 4 | **Battle of Guandu** | 200 | Cao Cao vs. Yuan Shao for the north. |
| 5 | **The Sleeping Dragon / Red Cliffs** | 207–208 | Liu Bei recruits Zhuge Liang; the iconic alliance vs. Cao Cao. The flagship "story" scenario. |
| 6 | **Three Kingdoms Established** | 220 | Wei, Shu, Wu formalized; the mature three-way war. |
| 7 | **The Northern Campaigns** | 228 | Zhuge Liang's expeditions; late-game, smaller rosters, high stakes. |
| + | **Hypothetical / Sandbox** | any | "What if" rosters, custom factions, randomized starts. |

## Two narrative modes

Players choose at campaign start how tightly history binds them:

- **Chronicle Mode (guided):** Scripted *Sanguo Yanyi* story events fire on
  historical cues (the Peach Garden Oath, Three Visits to the Thatched Cottage,
  the Empty Fort stratagem, the death of Guan Yu, etc.). Following them grants
  bonuses and cinematics; you experience the novel.
- **Free Mode (emergent):** Events are seeded but the world diverges freely
  based on player and AI action. History is a starting condition, not a script.
  Cao Cao might fall in 196; you might unify as a no-name officer.

## Campaign arc & structure

A campaign is the pursuit of the **Mandate of Heaven** — legitimacy to rule
all China. Structurally it moves through phases the systems gate naturally:

1. **Survival** — secure 1–2 cities, recruit a core of officers, stabilize
   food and order.
2. **Expansion** — annex neighbors via war, diplomacy, or vassalization.
3. **Consolidation** — administer a multi-province state; manage court factions,
   succession, and unrest.
4. **Hegemony** — one of the great powers; total-war scale battles, grand
   diplomacy, the Imperial seal.
5. **Unification** — claim the Mandate; declare a dynasty. **Win condition.**

### Win / loss conditions

- **Win:** Control all of China **or** reach a configurable territory/legitimacy
  threshold and proclaim your dynasty. Optional "historical victory" objectives
  per scenario (e.g., recreate the Shu-Han).
- **Lose:** Lose all territory, or (in ruler-death rules) your faction collapses
  with no valid heir.

## Story delivery

- **Set-piece events:** illustrated, voiced vignettes with branching choices
  (accept/refuse a sworn brotherhood; spare or execute a captured rival).
- **Character episodes:** personal questlines for marquee officers unlocked by
  relationships (recruiting Zhuge Liang, Guan Yu's loyalty arc, Lü Bu's betrayals).
- **Emergent chronicle:** the game auto-writes a readable "annals" log of your
  reign — battles, betrayals, marriages, deaths — that reads like a history book
  and is shareable.

## Tone & themes

Brotherhood and betrayal; loyalty vs. ambition; the cost of unification; the
fickleness of the Mandate of Heaven. Heroic but with real consequence — beloved
officers can and do die, and the game makes you feel it.
